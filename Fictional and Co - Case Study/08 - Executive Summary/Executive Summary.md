# Fictional & Co — Executive Summary

**Project:** SME Operations & Finance Improvement Case Study  
**Role:** Operations Transformation Manager  
**Business:** Fictional & Co  
**Location:** London  
**Sites:** 4  
**Annual Revenue:** £4.8m  
**Employees:** 65  

## Project Overview

Fictional & Co is a fictional multi-site hospitality SME created to demonstrate a practical approach to operations transformation, finance operations, cost control, process improvement and systems implementation.

The business operates four sites across London and is considering expansion to a fifth location.

The purpose of the project was to assess whether the current finance and operational model could support further growth and to identify practical improvements that would strengthen control, visibility and scalability.

---

## Business Challenge

Fictional & Co had grown from one location to four, but many of its finance and operational processes had remained manual or fragmented.

Key challenges included:

- POS data not integrated efficiently with financial reporting
- no central purchase-order process
- supplier invoices arriving through multiple channels
- incomplete supplier and contract records
- manual cash-flow forecasting
- labour performance reviewed mainly after month-end
- management reporting dependent on several systems and spreadsheets
- operational decisions spread across email and WhatsApp
- limited visibility of committed spend before invoices were received

The business did not require a complete systems replacement.

The main issue was that existing processes, responsibilities and systems were not working together consistently enough to support further growth.

---

## Approach

The project followed a structured transformation process:

1. Document the current operating model
2. Assess finance, operational and systems risks
3. Define the management KPI framework
4. Analyse financial and operational sample data
5. Build a management dashboard
6. Identify performance and cost exceptions
7. Redesign key workflows
8. Review systems and automation opportunities
9. Develop a phased 90-day implementation roadmap

---

## Diagnostic Findings

The diagnostic identified several priority areas.

### Purchasing and Cost Control

Site managers could order directly from approved suppliers without a central purchase-order or approval process.

This limited visibility of committed spend and made it harder to identify budget pressure before costs were incurred.

### Supplier Invoice Management

Invoices were received through multiple channels, increasing manual handling and the risk of missed, duplicated or delayed invoices.

### Supplier Management

Supplier pricing, payment terms, contract dates and ownership were not consistently maintained.

This reduced commercial visibility and limited the ability to manage supplier relationships proactively.

### Management Reporting

Reporting required manual consolidation from Xero, the POS system and several spreadsheets.

This created a retrospective reporting model rather than one designed for timely management action.

### Labour Visibility

Labour costs were primarily reviewed through monthly reporting.

This reduced the opportunity to correct rota, overtime or agency cost issues during the trading month.

### Scalability

The operating model relied heavily on individual knowledge, spreadsheets and manual communication.

Without stronger processes and ownership, Site 5 would increase administrative burden and control risk.

---

## Data Analysis

A fictional 12-month dataset was created across:

- revenue,
- cost of goods sold,
- labour,
- operating costs,
- suppliers,
- contracts,
- and supplier spend.

The analysis identified several material exceptions.

### Vauxhall

Vauxhall was the clearest priority site.

Key findings included:

- revenue approximately 4.3% below budget,
- higher COGS pressure,
- recurring labour overspend,
- agency labour costs,
- lower site contribution than the other locations,
- and elevated maintenance spend.

### Supplier Cost Control

Fresh Foods spend at Vauxhall was approximately 6.6% above baseline over the period.

Rapid Repairs spend at Vauxhall was approximately 27.4% above baseline, driven by repeated maintenance call-outs.

Camden also showed a later-period increase in beverage supplier spend.

### Positive Comparators

Borough remained broadly stable and performed close to or above plan.

Camden showed stronger revenue momentum and the highest site contribution in the group.

This reinforced the decision to use exception-based management rather than applying the same corrective actions to every location.

## KPI Framework

The project introduced a focused management KPI framework covering:

- Revenue vs Budget
- Gross Margin %
- Cost of Goods Sold %
- Labour Cost %
- Site Contribution
- Supplier Spend Variance
- Budget vs Actual Operating Costs
- 13-Week Cash Forecast
- Accounts Payable Ageing
- Contract / Renewal Exposure

KPIs were split between weekly operational review and monthly financial review.

The aim was to shift management from retrospective reporting towards earlier intervention.

---

## Process Improvements

Three core workflows were redesigned.

### Supplier Invoice Process

**Current state:** Multiple invoice channels, inconsistent approvals and manual handling.

**Future state:** Central invoice intake, defined approval ownership, structured validation, Xero integration and exception-based review.

### Purchasing Process

**Current state:** Direct site ordering with limited visibility of committed spend.

**Future state:** Delegated approval thresholds, lightweight PO / approved-spend controls, budget checks and stronger supplier pricing visibility.

### Management Reporting

**Current state:** Manual consolidation across several systems and spreadsheets.

**Future state:** Defined data ownership, standardised KPI calculation, exception reporting, management dashboard and action tracking.

---

## Systems & SaaS Recommendations

The review concluded that Fictional & Co did not require a large-scale technology replacement programme.

### Retain

- Xero
- Existing POS platform
- External payroll provider
- Google Drive
- Spreadsheet-based budgeting in the short term

### Improve

- supplier register,
- contract register,
- budgeting structure,
- management reporting,
- cash-flow inputs.

### Integrate

- POS and finance reporting,
- supplier data and reporting inputs.

### Automate

- invoice capture,
- invoice approval routing,
- contract renewal reminders,
- supplier variance alerts,
- selected reporting refresh processes.

Automation was recommended only after processes and data definitions had been standardised.

## 90-Day Roadmap

### Days 1–30 — Stabilise and Standardise

Focus on:

- dedicated invoice intake,
- supplier register,
- contract register,
- KPI ownership,
- Vauxhall priority review.

### Days 31–60 — Strengthen Controls and Visibility

Focus on:

- purchasing approvals,
- weekly management review,
- management dashboard,
- labour monitoring,
- supplier variance reporting.

### Days 61–90 — Automate and Prepare to Scale

Focus on:

- invoice workflow automation,
- contract renewal reminders,
- reporting automation,
- cash-flow process improvement,
- Site 5 readiness review.

## Expected Business Impact

The proposed operating model is designed to provide:

- earlier visibility of financial and operational issues,
- stronger cost control,
- clearer accountability,
- better supplier management,
- faster management reporting,
- reduced manual administration,
- improved auditability,
- and a more scalable operating model.

The project does not claim realised savings because it is a fictional case study.

The value lies in the diagnostic approach, business judgement, process design and implementation structure.
