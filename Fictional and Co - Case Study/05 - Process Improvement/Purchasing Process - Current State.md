# Fictional & Co — Purchasing Process: Current State

**Project:** SME Operations & Finance Improvement Case Study  
**Role:** Operations Transformation Manager  
**Process:** Purchasing and Purchase Approval

## Purpose

This document maps the current purchasing process at Fictional & Co before redesign.

The objective is to understand how purchasing decisions are made across the four sites, how supplier commitments are created, and where control or visibility gaps may arise.

---

## Current-State Workflow

1. Site Manager identifies a purchasing need.
2. Site Manager selects an approved supplier.
3. Order is placed directly with the supplier.
4. Goods or services are delivered.
5. Supplier invoice is issued.
6. Invoice enters the supplier invoice process.
7. Finance identifies the cost once the invoice is received and recorded.

## Pain Points Identified

### 1. No central purchase-order process

Orders are placed directly by site managers without a standard purchase-order workflow.

**Risk / impact:**
- Limited visibility of committed spend before invoicing
- Harder to compare actual spend against budget in real time
- Finance sees the cost later in the process

### 2. Limited approval structure

There is no consistent approval threshold based on spend value, category or business impact.

**Risk / impact:**
- Different sites may follow different purchasing behaviours
- Larger or unusual purchases may not receive appropriate review
- Accountability is less clear

### 3. Weak link between purchasing and supplier controls

Approved suppliers are used, but agreed pricing, contract terms and ownership are not consistently recorded.

**Risk / impact:**
- Purchases may be made without checking current agreed terms
- Pricing differences may be identified only after invoice receipt
- Supplier leverage across multiple sites may be underused

### 4. Spend visibility is retrospective

Committed spend is not centrally visible at the point of purchase.

**Risk / impact:**
- Budget overruns may be identified after the cost has already been incurred
- Cash-flow forecasting is less accurate
- Management has less time to intervene

### 5. Site-by-site purchasing can reduce group leverage

Individual sites may place similar orders independently.

**Risk / impact:**
- Missed opportunities for consolidated purchasing
- Inconsistent pricing across sites
- Reduced negotiating power with key suppliers

---

## Current-State Assessment

**Priority:** High

The current purchasing model provides flexibility to site managers but offers limited central visibility and control.

As Fictional & Co grows, the process may become increasingly difficult to manage without clearer approval rules, stronger supplier data and better visibility of committed spend.

---

## Improvement Objective

Create a proportionate purchasing process that:

- preserves operational speed,
- introduces clear approval thresholds,
- improves visibility of committed spend,
- strengthens supplier controls,
- and supports better budgeting and cash-flow planning.
