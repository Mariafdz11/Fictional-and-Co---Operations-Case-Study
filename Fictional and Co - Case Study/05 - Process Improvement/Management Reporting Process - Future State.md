# Fictional & Co — Management Reporting Process: Future State

**Project:** SME Operations & Finance Improvement Case Study  
**Role:** Operations Transformation Manager  
**Process:** Management Reporting

## Purpose

This document defines the proposed future-state management reporting process for Fictional & Co.

The redesigned process aims to improve reporting speed, consistency and decision usefulness while keeping the reporting model practical for an SME.

---

## Future-State Workflow

1. Standard data sources are defined for each KPI.
2. POS, Xero, labour and supplier data are captured using consistent structures.
3. Data is refreshed on an agreed weekly and monthly cadence.
4. Core KPIs are calculated consistently across all sites.
5. Material exceptions are flagged automatically or through defined thresholds.
6. Management dashboard is updated.
7. Finance and Operations review exceptions before the management meeting.
8. Actions are assigned to named owners.
9. Progress is reviewed at the next operating meeting.

## Proposed Reporting Structure

### Weekly Operating Review

Focus on metrics that management can still influence during the month:

- Revenue vs Budget
- Labour Cost %
- COGS %
- 13-Week Cash Forecast
- Accounts Payable Ageing
- Material operational exceptions

### Monthly Management Review

Focus on performance, trends and accountability:

- Revenue vs Budget
- Gross Margin %
- COGS %
- Labour Cost %
- Site Contribution
- Supplier Spend Variance
- Budget vs Actual Operating Costs
- Contract / Renewal Exposure

---

## Proposed Process Improvements

### 1. Standard data ownership

Each KPI should have a clearly defined source and owner.

**Expected benefit:**
- Reduced ambiguity
- More consistent reporting
- Easier troubleshooting

### 2. Standard reporting templates

Use consistent structures across sites and reporting periods.

**Expected benefit:**
- Faster preparation
- Easier comparison
- Reduced manual rework

### 3. Weekly operational visibility

Introduce a short weekly management view alongside the monthly pack.

**Expected benefit:**
- Earlier intervention
- Better labour and cost control
- More responsive decision-making

### 4. Exception-based reporting

Highlight only material variances or issues requiring management action.

**Expected benefit:**
- More focused meetings
- Faster decision-making
- Clearer priorities

### 5. Action tracking

Every material exception should record:

- issue,
- owner,
- agreed action,
- due date,
- status.

**Expected benefit:**
- Better accountability
- Stronger follow-through
- Clearer link between reporting and operational improvement

### 6. Reduce manual consolidation

Where practical, standardise imports, templates or integrations between source systems and reporting tools.

**Expected benefit:**
- Reduced finance admin
- Faster reporting cycles
- Lower risk of manual error

---

## Systems Approach

The objective is not necessarily to implement a new BI platform immediately.

A practical first phase could use:

- Xero,
- POS exports,
- structured Excel / Google Sheets,
- a standard management dashboard,
- and clear data ownership.

Further automation or BI tools can be considered once reporting definitions and processes are stable.

---

## Expected Impact

The redesigned reporting process should provide:

- faster access to management information,
- improved consistency across sites,
- stronger financial and operational visibility,
- earlier identification of cost issues,
- less manual consolidation,
- and clearer accountability for actions.

---

## Success Measures

The future-state reporting process can be monitored using:

- reporting cycle time,
- number of manual data preparation steps,
- number of unresolved reporting exceptions,
- percentage of KPIs updated on time,
- number of management actions overdue,
- and reduction in recurring unexplained variances.

---

## Process Improvement Summary

The three redesigned workflows — supplier invoices, purchasing and management reporting — create a stronger operating model around Fictional & Co's existing systems.

The next stage of the case study will assess where systems, SaaS and automation can support these redesigned processes.
