# Fictional & Co — Supplier Invoice Process: Current State

**Project:** SME Operations & Finance Improvement Case Study  
**Role:** Operations Transformation Manager  
**Process:** Supplier Invoice Management

## Purpose

This document maps the current supplier invoice process at Fictional & Co before process redesign.

The objective is to understand how invoices currently enter the business, how they are reviewed and approved, and where operational or control weaknesses may arise.

---

## Current-State Workflow

1. Supplier issues invoice.
2. Invoice is sent to one of several channels:
   - Finance email
   - Individual site email address
   - Supplier portal
3. Site manager confirms whether goods or services were received.
4. Finance & Admin Assistant receives or retrieves the invoice.
5. Invoice is entered into Xero.
6. Coding is reviewed by the Finance Manager before month-end.
7. Approved invoices are included in the payment process.

## Pain Points Identified

### 1. Multiple invoice intake channels

Invoices are received through finance email, site email addresses and supplier portals.

**Risk / impact:**
- Increased chance of missed invoices
- Duplicate processing risk
- More time spent locating documents
- Inconsistent audit trail

### 2. No standard invoice intake ownership

Responsibility for making sure all invoices reach finance is partly dependent on site managers and individual supplier behaviour.

**Risk / impact:**
- Invoices may be processed late
- Month-end liabilities may be incomplete
- Finance team spends time chasing missing documents

### 3. Approval evidence is inconsistent

Site managers confirm whether goods or services were received, but the approval process is not fully standardised.

**Risk / impact:**
- Limited visibility of who approved what
- Difficult to trace decisions later
- Approval delays can slow payment processing

### 4. Manual handling before accounting entry

Finance must retrieve, check and enter invoices from multiple sources.

**Risk / impact:**
- Administrative workload
- Increased opportunity for manual errors
- Limited scalability as supplier volume grows

### 5. Limited connection to purchasing controls

There is no central purchase-order process.

**Risk / impact:**
- Finance cannot consistently match invoices against an approved purchasing commitment
- Cost control happens later in the process
- Unexpected spend may only be identified after invoice receipt

---

## Current-State Assessment

**Priority:** High

The process works at the current scale but depends heavily on manual handling, individual knowledge and inconsistent communication channels.

As Fictional & Co considers a fifth site, the current process is likely to create additional administrative burden and weaker control unless invoice intake and approval are standardised.

---

## Improvement Objective

Create a single, traceable and scalable supplier invoice process that:

- standardises invoice intake,
- clarifies approval ownership,
- reduces manual chasing,
- improves auditability,
- supports stronger purchasing controls,
- and integrates cleanly with Xero.
