# Fictional & Co — Purchasing Process: Future State

**Project:** SME Operations & Finance Improvement Case Study  
**Role:** Operations Transformation Manager  
**Process:** Purchasing and Purchase Approval

## Purpose

This document defines the proposed future-state purchasing process for Fictional & Co.

The redesigned process aims to improve cost control and visibility without creating unnecessary administrative burden for site teams.

---

## Future-State Workflow

1. Site Manager identifies a purchasing need.
2. Purchase request is checked against:
   - approved supplier,
   - budget availability,
   - agreed pricing,
   - category rules,
   - approval threshold.
3. Low-value routine purchases proceed within delegated authority.
4. Higher-value or non-standard purchases are escalated for approval.
5. Purchase order or approved-spend reference is created where required.
6. Order is placed with the supplier.
7. Commitment becomes visible to Finance before invoice receipt.
8. Goods or services are received and confirmed.
9. Supplier invoice is matched to the approved purchase or PO.
10. Exceptions are flagged for review.

## Proposed Approval Model

A simple delegated-authority structure could be used.

| Purchase Type | Example Approval Route |
|---|---|
| Routine low-value purchase within budget | Site Manager |
| Higher-value purchase within budget | Operations Manager |
| Non-budgeted or unusual purchase | Operations Manager + Finance |
| Contractual or material commitment | Finance Manager / Managing Director |

The exact monetary thresholds would be defined based on Fictional & Co's risk appetite and transaction profile.

---

## Proposed Process Improvements

### 1. Delegated purchasing authority

Define which purchases can be approved at site level and which require escalation.

**Expected benefit:**
- Faster routine purchasing
- Clearer accountability
- Better control over higher-risk spend

### 2. Purchase request / PO reference

Introduce a lightweight purchase request or PO process for material spend.

**Expected benefit:**
- Visibility of commitments before invoice receipt
- Better invoice matching
- Improved audit trail

### 3. Budget check before commitment

Purchases are reviewed against available budget before approval where practical.

**Expected benefit:**
- Earlier identification of overspend
- Stronger budget ownership
- Better management intervention

### 4. Supplier pricing and terms check

Key purchases are checked against the supplier register and agreed commercial terms.

**Expected benefit:**
- Better pricing discipline
- Reduced risk of inconsistent site-level pricing
- Stronger supplier negotiation position

### 5. Group-level purchasing visibility

Purchasing data is reviewed across all sites rather than only site by site.

**Expected benefit:**
- Better supplier concentration analysis
- Consolidation opportunities
- Increased negotiating leverage

### 6. Exception-based control

Routine purchases within agreed rules should flow quickly, while unusual purchases are escalated.

**Expected benefit:**
- Control without excessive bureaucracy
- Management attention focused on material decisions
- More scalable process

---

## Systems Approach

The purchasing process should be designed around business requirements first.

Possible implementation options include:

- a structured purchase request form,
- a simple approval workflow,
- PO functionality within existing software,
- or a lightweight SaaS tool if transaction volume justifies it.

The objective is not to introduce a complex procurement platform, but to create enough control and visibility for a growing SME.

---

## Expected Impact

The redesigned process should provide:

- earlier visibility of committed spend,
- stronger budget control,
- clearer purchasing accountability,
- improved supplier pricing discipline,
- better invoice matching,
- and a more scalable operating model for Site 5.

---

## Success Measures

The future-state process can be monitored using:

- % of material purchases with approval before commitment
- % of invoices matched to PO / approved spend
- number of non-approved supplier purchases
- budget variance by site and category
- supplier price variance
- number of purchase exceptions requiring escalation
